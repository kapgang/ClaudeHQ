# Qualifying/Non-Qualifying Classification Helper

## Instructions

Guide the process of determining and applying Q/NQ percentages:

1. Understand what's being classified (roles, departments, expenses, activities)
2. Apply R&D credit qualifying criteria
3. Suggest appropriate Q% based on nature of work
4. Document reasoning for audit trail
5. Flag edge cases for ET review

## R&D Credit Qualifying Criteria (Section 41)

### Four-Part Test for Qualified Research

For an activity to qualify, it must meet ALL four parts:

1. **Permitted Purpose**
   - New or improved business component (product, process, software, technique, formula, invention)
   - Functional improvements (performance, reliability, quality)
   - NOT: Aesthetic, cosmetic, or style changes

2. **Technological in Nature**
   - Relies on principles of physical/biological sciences, computer science, or engineering
   - NOT: Social sciences, business processes, market research

3. **Elimination of Uncertainty**
   - Uncertainty about capability, method, or appropriate design
   - NOT: Uncertainty about market demand or financial viability

4. **Process of Experimentation**
   - Systematic trial and error, modeling, simulation, or testing
   - Evaluation of alternatives
   - NOT: Routine or ordinary development

## Qualifying Activity Categories

### Highly Qualifying (75-100%)

**Software Development (Core Engineering)**
- Developing new algorithms
- Performance optimization requiring technical innovation
- Integration of new technologies
- Solving complex technical problems
- Architecture design for scalability

**Product Engineering**
- Designing new products or features
- Prototyping and testing
- CAD/CAM design work
- Materials research and testing
- Technical feasibility studies

**Process Engineering**
- Developing new manufacturing processes
- Automation design and implementation
- Process optimization with technical innovation
- Equipment customization and testing

**Scientific Research**
- Laboratory research and experimentation
- Clinical trials (with uncertainty)
- Formula development
- Testing and validation

### Moderately Qualifying (25-75%)

**Technical Management**
- Engineering managers overseeing qualified projects
- Technical project planning
- Architecture review and guidance
- % based on time allocation to qualifying vs non-qualifying projects

**Mixed Roles**
- QA engineers (testing novel functionality: higher %; routine testing: lower %)
- DevOps (infrastructure innovation vs maintenance)
- Technical writers (documenting new R&D vs standard documentation)
- Data analysts (developing new models vs routine reporting)

**Support Functions with Technical Contribution**
- IT supporting R&D systems
- Technical specialists providing direct support
- Lab technicians conducting experiments

### Minimally/Non-Qualifying (0-25%)

**Administrative Functions**
- HR, finance, legal, marketing
- General business operations
- Routine maintenance
- Customer support (unless contributing to product improvement R&D)

**Non-Technical Management**
- Business managers without direct technical contribution
- Executive leadership (unless hands-on in R&D)

**Routine Activities**
- Routine data entry
- Standard testing (no innovation)
- Documentation of existing processes
- Customer service

## Department-Level Q% Guidelines

### Common Department Classifications

| Department | Typical Q% | Rationale |
|------------|-----------|-----------|
| R&D - Core | 90-100% | Primary research function |
| Engineering | 75-90% | Mostly qualified design work, some non-R&D tasks |
| Product Development | 70-85% | Mix of innovation and routine development |
| Software Development | 60-80% | Varies by project type (new features vs maintenance) |
| QA/Testing | 40-70% | Testing novel functionality higher; routine lower |
| IT/Infrastructure | 20-50% | Depends on innovation vs maintenance |
| DevOps | 30-60% | Automation innovation vs routine operations |
| Technical Support | 10-30% | Mostly non-qualifying unless contributing to R&D |
| Management | 0-50% | Based on direct technical contribution |
| Sales/Marketing | 0-5% | Generally non-qualifying |
| HR/Finance/Admin | 0% | Administrative functions |

**Note:** These are guidelines. Actual percentages depend on specific job duties and time allocation.

## Time Allocation Methodology

### Direct vs Indirect Method

**Direct Time Tracking**
- Best: Employees track time to qualifying projects
- Use actual hours on R&D activities
- Calculate Q% = Qualifying Hours / Total Hours

**Estimation Method**
- Interview managers/employees about typical time allocation
- Document typical week's activities
- Apply consistent methodology across similar roles

## Classification Decision Framework

### Questions to Ask

**For Roles:**
1. What does this person do day-to-day?
2. Are they solving technical problems with uncertainty?
3. Are they experimenting or testing new approaches?
4. Do they directly contribute to new/improved products or processes?
5. What % of their time is spent on qualifying activities?

**For Departments:**
1. What is this department's primary function?
2. What projects are they working on?
3. How much is innovation vs maintenance/support?
4. Are they creating new capabilities or sustaining existing ones?

**For Expenses:**
1. What activity was this expense supporting?
2. Was it for qualified research activities?
3. Direct vs indirect relationship to R&D?

## Edge Cases and Red Flags

### Require ET Review

**High Q% for Unexpected Roles**
- "Administrative Assistant - 80% qualifying"
- Flag: Unlikely unless heavily involved in technical work
- Action: Document specific qualifying activities

**Inconsistent Within Similar Roles**
- "Software Engineer A: 90%, Software Engineer B: 40%"
- Question: Why the difference? Different projects?
- Action: Document rationale or normalize

**100% Qualifying**
- Any role at 100% should be scrutinized
- Even R&D scientists have some admin time
- Consider: Vacation, training, meetings, admin
- Typical max: 90-95% for pure R&D roles

**Department Q% Doesn't Match Industry Norms**
- "QA Department: 95%"
- Unusual: QA typically 40-70%
- Action: Document unique circumstances or adjust

### Documentation Requirements

For audit defense, document:
- **Methodology:** How Q% was determined
- **Source:** Time tracking data, interviews, project analysis
- **Assumptions:** Any estimates or judgments made
- **Review:** ET approval of approach
- **Consistency:** Applied uniformly across similar roles

## Q/NQ Application Workflow

### Step 1: Classify at Highest Level Practical

**Options:**
1. **Individual employee level** (most accurate, most work)
2. **Role/title level** (good balance)
3. **Department level** (simplest, least precise)

**Recommendation:** Role level within department
- Allows different Q% for "Software Engineer" in R&D vs IT
- More defensible than pure department level

### Step 2: Gather Supporting Data

**Time Tracking Data**
- If available, use actual hours by project
- Calculate % to qualifying projects

**Project Classifications**
- List all projects/initiatives
- Classify each as qualifying or non-qualifying
- Allocate employee time based on project participation

**Manager Interviews**
- Ask managers to estimate team's time on R&D
- Document in memo format for files

### Step 3: Apply Q% to Base Amounts

**Wage Base:**
```
Qualified Wages = Base Wages × Q%

Example:
Employee: John Doe
Role: Software Engineer - Product Team
Base W2: $120,000
Q%: 75%
Qualified Amount: $90,000
```

**Supply Base:**
```
Qualified Supplies = Total Supplies × Q%

Example:
Department: R&D Lab
Lab supplies: $50,000
Q%: 90% (mostly for experiments)
Qualified Amount: $45,000
```

### Step 4: QA and Validation

**Reasonability Checks:**
- Does total Q% of workforce align with industry?
- Are high-Q% roles truly spending that much time on R&D?
- Does qualified amount seem reasonable given company's R&D focus?

**Spot Check:**
- Review sample of high Q% employees
- Verify job descriptions support classification
- Confirm with managers
